# Revel Gifts: consolidated research findings

Research synthesis • 31 October 2026

## Scope and reading guide

This synthesis combines four sequential independent research reports: [gift-giving psychology](01-gifting-psychology.md), [recipient profiling](02-recipient-profiling.md), [agent recommendations](03-agent-recommendations.md), and [privacy and social context](04-privacy-social-context.md). Each later report critically reviewed the earlier work. This is a targeted research review, not a systematic review or a finalized product schema. The empirical results below concern studied populations and choices; the proposed Revel design and thresholds remain hypotheses for testing.

**Recommendation:** Define the Minimum Useful Profile (MUP) as an occasion-relative decision state, not a list of mandatory fields. A single recent, specific, authorized preference can support a meaningful direction. Whether any particular gift is ready to buy additionally depends on its constraints, the giver's circumstances, and current availability. Keep known unknowns visible and ask only questions that could change the decision.

## A. Evidence-backed gifting principles

1. **Honor expressed wishes.** Across five studies, recipients appreciated requested gifts more than unrequested alternatives, while givers underestimated that advantage. A wish is useful evidence, not an obligation or proof that surprise is unwelcome. [Gino and Flynn, 2011](https://static.tumblr.com/5tnincw/jCap15er9/gifts-explicit-ginoflynn-2011.pdf).
2. **Design for use after the exchange.** Gift-giving research identifies a giver–recipient mismatch between the appeal of presenting a gift and its usefulness in the recipient's life. Practical fit, effort and flexibility matter, although an explanatory review is not proof of one universal mechanism. [Galak, Givi and Williams, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721416656937); [Steffel, Williams and LeBoeuf](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Steffel-Williams-LeBoeuf-April-2015.pdf).
3. **A distinctive or expensive gift is not automatically better.** Experiments found that givers sometimes chose distinct gifts for multiple people even when repeating one would have better matched preferences; other studies found givers overestimated the link between price and appreciation. [Steffel and LeBoeuf, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1086/674199); [Flynn and Adams, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2008.11.003).
4. **Meaning is valuable when supported by actual context.** Studies found recipients more interested in sentimental gifts than givers expected in tested comparisons. A supported personal note can add meaning to a useful gift; an invented shared story cannot. [Givi and Galak, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2017.06.002).
5. **Experiences are an option, not a default winner.** Real-exchange studies found experiential gifts strengthened recipient-reported relationship outcomes more than material gifts in the studied settings, including experiences not shared with the giver. They do not establish universal preference or remove scheduling, access and travel burdens. [Chan and Mogilner, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucw067).
6. **Context changes the decision.** Relationship, occasion and individual customs influence meaning and obligations. Research is uneven across cultures, families and occasions; nationality, age or relationship labels are poor substitutes for individual evidence. [Freling et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21981).
7. **Ask questions that change a choice.** Conversational recommendation research supports targeted elicitation in other domains; a restaurant study used simulations and data from 28 participants, so it does not validate a gifting question count. [Christakopoulou et al., 2016](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2016/papers/files/rfp0063-christakopoulouA.pdf).
8. **Measure recipient outcomes, not just a persuasive explanation.** Explanation framing can affect selections, and a convincing rationale is not evidence of fit. Test recipient-rated fit and later use as well as giver confidence and friction. [Balog, Radlinski and Petrov, 2023](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-impact-of-explanation-bias-a-study-of-natural-language-justifications-for-recommender-systems/).

## B. Ideal Gifting Profile

These categories describe potentially useful information, not a mandatory intake or schema. Store the least needed for an authorized purpose. A category can be irrelevant to a particular gift.

| Category | Value to a decision | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Current wishes and explicit exclusions | Direct evidence of desired and unwanted options; check whether a wish is still current or already fulfilled. | Essential when relevant and available |
| Candidate-specific constraints | Fit and safety: e.g. a format, compatibility requirement, food exclusion, location or schedule. Record an actionable constraint without its sensitive cause. | Essential when the candidate depends on it |
| Interests, activities and usage | Gives direction and helps distinguish a concrete current activity from a generic hobby. | High value |
| Existing possessions and duplication | Avoids redundant or incompatible purchases and identifies a welcome upgrade. | High value when applicable |
| Practical and gift preferences | Preference for an experience, physical object, flexible choice or surprise; tolerance for effort, space and travel. | High value when relevant |
| Style and specifications | Color, material, size, format or brand refine a candidate when the person wants to share them. | Optional unless a candidate requires them |
| Personal meaning, customs and relationships | Authorized shared memories or traditions can support appropriate personal meaning. | Optional and context-dependent |
| Feedback and changes over time | Recipient-approved corrections, enjoyment and use can improve future suggestions; silence or polite thanks is not proof of success. | Optional |

Each retained claim needs appropriate provenance, date or freshness, direct/observed/inferred status, qualitative confidence, purpose and audience. This metadata itself can be sensitive; restrict detailed provenance. Preserve conflicts instead of silently merging them. A recent recipient-authored wish usually deserves more weight than an older third-party inference, but even an explicit wish may be stale. Giver-supplied observations remain attributed to the giver and do not automatically become recipient-verified shared facts.

## C. Minimum Useful Profile and readiness

**Essential for a meaningfully personalized direction:** at least one relevant, sufficiently specific and authorized positive signal, such as a recent wish or concrete activity; a known source and enough freshness/context to interpret it; and an authorized way to use it for this request. One strong preference can suffice. “Wants this paperback,” for example, is stronger than “likes books.” With only a vague signal, offer clearly labeled exploratory directions, not a claimed strong match. No authorized signal means the giver can still explore general ideas or volunteer their own observations; it does not justify pretending to know the recipient.

**Essential for an actual candidate:** no known conflict with an exclusion, plus resolution of any critical safety, compatibility, fit or access uncertainty. Unknown dislikes are **unknown**, not evidence that there are none. Ask a targeted question when it changes candidate safety or suitability, or withhold that candidate and offer a less constrained direction. A general “anything to avoid?” can help, but cannot prove every restriction is absent. A sensitive exclusion can be stated as an actionable boundary without recording a diagnosis.

**Giver context:** occasion, budget, deadline and delivery or location are request context, not permanent recipient attributes. Stated provisional defaults can support exploration; confirm what matters before a purchase. Candidate purchase readiness also requires current checks of total price, availability, delivery or booking terms, and any relevant ownership, size, format or access details.

**Confidence and recency:** label evidence as direct, observed or inferred and consider its date and situation; do not invent numerical confidence. A former interest or contradictory claim calls for clarification if the difference would affect the choice. Avoid repeatedly interrogating someone about inconsequential gaps.

Use three useful states: **exploratory** (broad or uncertain direction), **promising** (a supported idea with an identified outstanding check), and **purchase-ready** (positive support, critical constraints resolved, and relevant logistics verified). Ask another question when plausible answers would change the shortlist or prevent a consequential mistake and the question is worth the burden. If a critical unknown remains after onboarding, withhold that option or seek clarification; a question cap does not override the gate. These states are proposed product criteria, not validated scientific thresholds.

## D. Adaptive onboarding

1. Establish which assistant and facts the recipient has authorized for this purpose and audience. Existing assistant access to a memory does not grant permission to export it. Retrieve only relevant, permitted summaries, keeping raw conversation and third-party details out of the handoff where possible.
2. Identify the occasion and the giver's practical constraints separately. Review the available claims for directness, freshness, conflicts and permission. Do not repeat adequately answered questions.
3. Ask one question at a time, allowing “skip” and “I don't know.” Rank questions by whether answers would alter candidate eligibility or order, the consequence of error, likelihood the person knows, and sensitivity and effort. Rerank after each answer. A critical constraint can move ahead of a wish question.
4. Default question opportunities, reordered to suit the decision: (a) recent wish or shared wish list; (b) relevant exclusions or must-have fit; (c) specific activity or how the recipient would use a gift; (d) candidate-specific ownership, format, schedule or access; (e) authorized shared meaning or surprise preference.
5. Stop when supported choices are possible or further questions will not materially change them. Aim for zero to three, with at most five initial discovery questions after authorized existing knowledge; ask none if sufficient information already exists. This cap and typical range are product hypotheses to test, not findings from the literature. Checkout questions and genuinely necessary safety checks are separate; unresolved critical constraints still block the affected candidate.

For multiple assistants, give each a separate scoped, revocable grant. An assistant contributes a short, attributable, permitted claim, not wholesale memory exports. Resolve material disagreements with the recipient where possible; do not treat repeated inference as independent confirmation. The recipient can review, correct and revoke sharing within the service's actual technical limits.

## E. Recommendation workflow

1. **Frame:** obtain or provisionally state occasion, budget, deadline and delivery context; identify the verified giver.
2. **Authorize and assemble:** evaluate current audience membership and permission for each relevant fact and assistant, then retrieve the minimum permitted context. Identity alone confers no access. Keep request data distinct from recipient profile data.
3. **Assess:** classify evidence by source, freshness and conflict; identify plausible directions, relevant exclusions and the highest-value missing fact. Ask selectively or proceed with labeled uncertainty.
4. **Generate:** use the existing personal assistant to propose a small, meaningfully varied set grounded in what may be used. Treat unsourced ideas as concepts. An explicit current wish is a strong leading candidate where feasible.
5. **Gate and compare:** eliminate contradictions and hold candidates with critical unknowns. Compare recipient fit, practical use, occasion and relationship meaning, feasibility, evidence quality and permitted disclosure. Do not use a fabricated happiness score or let a compelling explanation outweigh a constraint.
6. **Explain and verify:** show a permitted concrete reason, a material tradeoff and any outstanding check. Check current listings, price including fees, availability, delivery and applicable terms before treating a candidate as purchase-ready. A suggestion, query or refusal can itself reveal a private fact; suppress it when disclosure is unauthorized, even if the explanation is vague.
7. **Refine and learn:** the giver chooses; corrections can change the shortlist. Invite recipient feedback after the occasion only with permission, and retain it only for its defined purpose. No response is missing data.

**Sparse example:** for a $40 birthday gift, the giver knows only that the recipient likes cooking. Ask whether there is a recent wish or a specific cooking activity. If unknown, offer clearly labeled exploratory directions, perhaps a flexible contribution or an invitation to share a wish, and verify willingness and terms before purchase. Do not infer a diet or claim a specific gadget is a fit.

**Rich example:** a recipient has recently authorized sharing a wish for a named paperback; the budget is $35 and the birthday is next week. Lead with the requested book, verify that it has not already been obtained if uncertain, and check a real listing's delivered price and estimated arrival. Explain the shareable wish and remaining checks. If unavailable in time, it remains a supported direction, not a purchase-ready recommendation.

## F. Product recommendations

### Hackathon MVP

- Make an assistant-led flow usable with a small, consented profile, giver request context, a few targeted questions and clear exploratory/promising/ready distinctions. Support a useful general fallback when no profile is shared.
- Capture source, freshness, directness, permission and audience for retained claims; allow unknown, correction and removal. Keep giver observations separate from recipient-authorized facts. Implement actual enforcement before claiming an audience or revocation works.
- Demonstrate a scoped assistant contribution and authenticated request with the verified requester's identity. Use existing audience labels with clear recipient-controlled semantics. Do not disclose occasion or candidate by default merely to seek guidance; acknowledge that even asking for gift guidance can signal a surprise.
- Block known exclusions and unresolved critical constraints. Produce short, evidence-grounded explanations and show what still needs verification. Do not invent product listings or purchase outcomes.
- Default sensitive details and managed contacts to restricted handling; avoid collecting diagnoses or unnecessary third-party memories. Define and disclose retention and deletion behavior actually implemented. For any pilot involving real people, assess applicable notice, consent and children's requirements before collecting their data.
- Run a small adversarial case set: stale wishes, conflicting claims, revoked permission, unverified requester, unknown critical constraint, a revealing recommendation, unsupported listing, and managed-contact authority. Review actual disclosure, not just displayed explanations.

### Post-hackathon improvements

- Test with consenting giver–recipient pairs across occasions and relationships. Where feasible compare adaptive assistant-seeded onboarding, a short static questionnaire and an ordinary-assistant baseline. Predefine recipient-rated fit as the primary outcome; track later use, unsuitable/duplicate gifts, question burden, privacy comfort, unauthorized disclosures, and missing feedback. Small samples yield exploratory findings, not definitive subgroup claims.
- Build recipient review of per-fact visibility, active grants and disclosures; apply current authorization on every request and make revocation/correction behavior observable. Implement and test deletion and downstream-provider handling, including realistic limits on recalling already copied information.
- Validate real assistant integrations, delegated authority, provider retention and current product retrieval; add concrete retention schedules and safe conflict resolution. Obtain legal review for target jurisdictions and third-party notice timing.

### Longer-term capabilities

- With authorization, enrich preferences over time using recipient feedback and multiple independently scoped assistants, while reviewing staleness and avoiding duplicate inference.
- Explore decision-aware question selection and ranking only if measured benefits justify complexity; keep recipient controls, evidence and hard constraints independent of any model score.
- Develop age-appropriate managed-contact transitions, contested-authority handling, richer recipient controls and broader cultural evaluation. Optional semantic retrieval or the proposed technology stack should follow demonstrated need and privacy review, not be treated as a research finding.

## G. Research limitations and unresolved questions

The underlying gift experiments include hypothetical choices and convenience samples as well as some real exchanges. Effects differ by relationship, occasion, type of gift and population; they do not prove a particular profile, model, question count or commercial workflow improves gifts. Recommender studies mostly concern other domains, simulations or offline datasets. Contextual integrity is an influential framework for reasoning about information flows, not proof that any specific permission interface works. Detailed study methods, caveats and additional primary links are in the four reports.

Open questions for product discussion and research: How much useful context can existing assistants actually contribute under their permissions and provider terms? Which audience and request controls do recipients understand? Does a shared request reveal too much about a surprise? How often does one preference produce a recipient-rated good gift? Which constraints can be verified without burdensome questions? How do usefulness and privacy differ for low-information profiles, families and different cultural contexts? How should managed contacts transition to subject control? What retention, third-party notice and children's protections apply in target markets?

Relevant primary sources include the gifting studies linked above, the [contextual integrity framework](https://digitalcommons.law.uw.edu/wlr/vol79/iss1/10/), the [GDPR text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679), [UK ICO guidance on notice](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/individual-rights/right-to-be-informed/), and the [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). Legal applicability depends on the actual service, data flows and jurisdiction; product consent is not a substitute for that analysis. Surprise alone does not establish an exception to third-party notice obligations.
